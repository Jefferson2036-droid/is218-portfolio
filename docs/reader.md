# Reader

Re-done 2026-11-23. Two reader hypotheses compared; one chosen. These are assumptions about readers, not facts about every recruiter.

| | A: Early-career reviewer for applied data/AI roles (chosen) | B: Engineering lead at one AI company |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Someone screening students and early-career candidates for applied data, AI or forward-deployed-style roles, in any industry (bank, AI company, consultancy) | A senior engineer at one FDE-style company deciding on a candidate |
| What they decide | Can this person learn fast, build something that works, work with the people who use it, and handle data responsibly? | Can this person own a hard technical problem for a customer? |
| Time spent | About a minute on the home page; a few minutes on one project if the first screen earns it | Several minutes on one deep project |
| What they need first | What kind of work I want, my strongest project with a link to try it, how I checked it, my experience, résumé | One deep case study and its code |
| What makes them leave | No clear direction, no way to try anything, buried résumé, broken links, unclear what I did versus my team | Shallow projects, no code |

## Decision
**Primary reader:** A. It keeps the site general so it works for banks, AI companies and other industries, which matches keeping my options open.

**Promise:** For an early-career reviewer, this portfolio makes it easy to see that I build data and AI tools with the people who use them — through my projects, my banking work and how I check my results — and to open my résumé or reach me in one click.
